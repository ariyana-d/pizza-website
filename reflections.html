Reflections

Why Pizza

I chose Pizza because I felt drawn to her at the shelter in a way I didn’t expect. She was fearful and distant, but I kept thinking about her after I left. Something about her situation stayed with me, and I wanted to see how she would change once she finally felt safe.

Early Experience

In the beginning, most of what I did was observation rather than interaction. Pizza would hide or stay far back whenever people were nearby, so the goal was just to let her exist without pressure. Any movement toward her had to be slow, because sudden actions would cause her to retreat. Over time, she began recognizing routines and became less reactive, even if she still kept her distance. The progress was small, but noticeable.

Final Note

This experience shows that progress with animals like Pizza doesn’t happen quickly. It takes patience, consistency, and respect for boundaries. Even small changes can build into a complete transformation over time.

Key Takeaways

  • Trust takes time
  • Consistency matters
  • Progress is gradual

Personal Reflection

I wanted to be a vet growing up, but I started realizing the reality of the job isn’t really what I thought it was, especially when it comes to pay and how much stress is involved. Even though I still care a lot about animals, I don’t think that path is the right fit for me anymore.

But working through Pizza’s journey kind of shifted things for me in a different direction. It made me more interested in rehabilitation and what it actually takes to help feral or scared animals adjust and feel safe again. Watching that slow change up close made it feel more real than anything I’ve read or seen before.

Now I find myself wanting to learn more about that side of things, how to properly work with feral cats, how shelters handle them, and what kind of patience and routines actually make a difference.

I’ve even thought about volunteering at a shelter just to get more experience and see how I can help in a real way, not just from the outside.
